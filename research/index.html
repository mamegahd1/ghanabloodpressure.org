Hypertension Prevalence and Burden in Ghana

These studies document the scale of the hypertension crisis in Ghana and establish the epidemiological foundation for the Initiative's intervention.

Meta-Analysis

Prevalence, awareness and control of hypertension in Ghana: A systematic review and meta-analysis

Bosu WK, Bosu DK. PLOS ONE. 2021;16(3):e0248137. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0248137. PMID: 33667277.
Read the full study →
Study summarySummary
A systematic review and meta-analysis of 85 articles covering 82,045 people aged 15 to 100 across Ghana. It finds that more than one in four adults in Ghana has hypertension (pooled prevalence 27.0%, 95% CI 24.0% to 30.0%). Of those with hypertension, 35% were aware of it, 22% were on treatment, and 6% had their blood pressure under control. These figures define the treatment gap that the Initiative addresses.
Relevance to the Initiative: The source of the more-than-1-in-4 prevalence figure and of the awareness, treatment, and control figures that anchor our pilot design.
Perspective

The Ghana heart initiative - a health system strengthening approach as index intervention model to solving Ghana's cardiovascular disease burden

Doku AK, Tetteh J, Edzeame J, Peters RJG, Agyemang C, Otchi EH, Yawson AE. Frontiers in Public Health. 2024;12:1330708. doi:10.3389/fpubh.2024.1330708. PMID: 38694980.
Read the full article →
Study summarySummary
A perspective article describing the Ghana Heart Initiative, a national strategy to strengthen the health system for cardiovascular care. It reports that cardiovascular diseases have become the leading causes of death in Ghana, primarily because of undetected and undertreated hypertension, and calls hypertension the single most preventable risk factor for cardiovascular disease in Ghana.
Relevance to the Initiative: Places undetected and undertreated hypertension at the center of Ghana's leading causes of death, which is the problem the Initiative works on.

Ghana Statistical Service (GSS) and ICF. 2024. Accra, Ghana, and Rockville, Maryland, USA: GSS and ICF.

Nationally representative household survey with results for urban and rural areas and for each of the 16 regions. It provides demographic and health indicators for Ghana. The 2022 round measured height and weight, anemia, and malaria, not blood pressure.

Regional Screening

Burden of hypertension in Ghana: analysis of awareness and screening campaign in the Ashanti Region of Ghana

Tannor EK, Nyarko OO, Adu-Boakye Y, Owusu Konadu S, Opoku G, Ankobea-Kokroe F, Opare Addo M, Amuzu EX, Ansah GJ, Appiah-Boateng K, Ansong D. JRSM Cardiovascular Disease. 2022;11:20480040221075521. doi:10.1177/20480040221075521. PMID: 35111304.
Read the full study →
Study summarySummary
Analysis of the May Measurement Month (MMM) 2018 awareness and screening campaign in the Ashanti Region of Ghana, conducted under the International Society of Hypertension's global MMM initiative. Enrolled 5,054 participants and identified a 37.4% hypertension prevalence, with 66.8% of those with high blood pressure having no prior history of hypertension. Participants were recruited opportunistically in the Kumasi metropolis, and the authors caution that the results do not represent the whole population.
Relevance to the Initiative: Shows how many undiagnosed cases a community screening campaign in Ghana can find: two in three of the people with high readings had no history of hypertension.
Case-Control Study

Dominant modifiable risk factors for stroke in Ghana and Nigeria (SIREN): a case-control study

Owolabi MO, Sarfo F, Akinyemi R, Gebregziabher M, Akpa O, Akpalu A, Wahab K, Obiako R, Owolabi L, Ovbiagele B; SIREN Team. Lancet Global Health. 2018;6(4):e436-e446. doi:10.1016/S2214-109X(18)30002-0. PMID: 29496511.
Read the full study →
Study summarySummary
At the time of publication, the largest study of stroke in Africa: a case-control study at 15 sites in Ghana and Nigeria with 2,118 stroke cases and 2,118 matched controls. Eleven potentially modifiable risk factors were associated with 98.2% of the population-attributable risk of stroke. Hypertension had by far the highest, at 90.8%. A case-control study shows association; it cannot prove cause.
Relevance to the Initiative: Identifies hypertension as the leading modifiable risk factor for stroke across its Ghanaian and Nigerian sites. This is the evidence behind the Initiative's line "Stroke is not sudden. We can prevent it."
Observational Study

Time delays in emergency stroke care in a low-resource referral hospital in Ghana

Yakubu HA, Marfo RO, Boakye-Yiadom J, Aidoo FM, Sarfo FS, Oteng RA. African Journal of Emergency Medicine. 2025;15(3):100882. doi:10.1016/j.afjem.2025.05.006. PMID: 40520040.
Read the full study →
Study summarySummary
An analysis of prospectively collected stroke registry data at Komfo Anokye Teaching Hospital (KATH) in Kumasi. Among 86 patients with CT-confirmed stroke, the median time from stroke onset to arrival at the emergency department was 35.3 hours (IQR 12.3 to 79.5), far beyond the 4.5-hour window for thrombolysis in ischemic stroke. It is a single-hospital study, and the authors caution that the results cannot be generalized.
Relevance to the Initiative: The study does not examine hypertension. Read alongside SIREN, it shows what is at stake once a stroke happens: at this hospital, half of the patients arrived more than 35 hours after onset. The Initiative's mandate is to find people before that clock starts.
Pillar 1

Health Education and Behavior Change

The Initiative's education model is built on the Patient Activated Learning System (PALS), developed by Dr. Monika Safford and now housed at MedExplain Health, and a Training of Trainers approach adapted from a curriculum for religious leaders developed in Mwanza, Tanzania.

Pilot Randomized Trial

Evaluation of the Patient Activated Learning System (PALS) to improve knowledge acquisition, retention, and medication decision making among hypertensive adults: Results of a pilot randomized controlled trial

Carmel AS, Cornelius-Schecter A, Frankel B, Jannat-Khah D, Sinha S, Pelzman F, Safford MM. Patient Education and Counseling. 2019;102(8):1467-1474. doi:10.1016/j.pec.2019.03.001. PMID: 30928344.
Read the full study →
Study summarySummary
A pilot randomized trial of PALS. It compared PALS with WebMD pages about one blood pressure medication among 104 adults with hypertension. PALS users scored higher on knowledge immediately afterwards, and more of them said they would feel comfortable taking the medication. Recall after one week and trust were similar in the two groups. PALS integrates Adult Learning Theory, Social Cognitive Theory, Bartle's Taxonomy, and storytelling, structured around Reusable Knowledge Objects (RKOs).
Relevance to the Initiative: PALS is the core methodology adapted for our community health education curriculum at all screening and church-based events.
Multi-Site Trial

Practice Facilitation and Peer Coaching for Uncontrolled Hypertension Among Black Individuals: A Randomized Clinical Trial

Safford MM, Cummings DM, Halladay JR, et al. JAMA Internal Medicine. 2024;184(5):538-546. doi:10.1001/jamainternmed.2024.0047. PMID: 38497987.
Read the full study →
Study summarySummary
Cluster-randomized trial of 1,592 Black patients with persistently uncontrolled hypertension at 69 rural primary care practices in the US Southeast. While the overall result was null, the subgroup of participants under age 60 receiving peer coaching showed approximately 5 mmHg greater systolic BP reduction than enhanced usual care, a benefit comparable to a low-dose antihypertensive medication. Informs the Initiative's design choice to integrate peer-delivered education and tracking with younger working-age populations.
Relevance to the Initiative: Informs the Initiative's design choice to integrate peer-delivered education with younger working-age populations. The under-60 finding is a subgroup result from rural practices in the United States. It suggests, and does not prove, that peer coaching may help the working-age adults the Initiative aims to reach: trotro drivers, market women, and their families.
Curriculum Development

Development of a curriculum to educate religious leaders about blood pressure using community-based participatory research and educational theory in Mwanza, Tanzania

Cohen EY, Kavishe BB, Urry M, Okello E, Kapiga S, Mwakisole AH, Kalokola F, Malibwa D, Peck RN, Downs JA. BMC Medical Education. 2025;25(1):265. doi:10.1186/s12909-025-06836-1. PMID: 39966863.
Read the full study →
Study summarySummary
Curriculum development paper from Weill Cornell Medicine and partners in Mwanza, Tanzania. The team, which included religious leaders as trainers, developed a curriculum to teach local religious leaders to address and screen their communities for hypertension, using a Community Based Participatory Research framework and evidence-based educational strategies (Kern's framework for medical education, Bloom's learning domains, and Knowles adult learning principles). The paper describes how the curriculum was developed and reports no outcome data. This curriculum is the structural source for the Initiative's adaptation.
Relevance to the Initiative: Direct methodological precedent. The Initiative's Training of Trainers curriculum adapts materials developed in this project for the Greater Accra context.

Religious leaders, physicians fight hypertension in Tanzania and beyond

Stroke Warning Signs

BE-FAST Stroke Warning Signs

American Stroke Association. Evaluated in: Aroor S, Singh R, Goldstein LB. Stroke. 2017;48(2):479-481. doi:10.1161/STROKEAHA.116.015169. PMID: 28082668.
See the warning signs →
Study summarySummary
The BE-FAST mnemonic (Balance, Eyes, Face, Arm, Speech, Time) adds balance loss and vision changes to the older FAST warning signs. In a review of 736 patients admitted with ischemic stroke at one United States center, 14.1% had none of the FAST symptoms; counting balance, leg, and vision symptoms as well reduced that share to 4.4%. The American Stroke Association now teaches B.E. F.A.S.T. as its warning signs of stroke.
Relevance to the Initiative: BE-FAST is integrated into our community education sessions, empowering participants to recognize stroke symptoms and act immediately.
Pillars 2 & 3

Screening, Treatment, and Follow-Up Models

The Initiative's clinical protocol and 12-month follow-up system are grounded in WHO frameworks and in published implementation models from community hypertension programs.

Technical Package

World Health Organization.
Access the HEARTS package →
Study summarySummary
The WHO's technical package for cardiovascular disease management in primary health care. HEARTS has six modules: Healthy-lifestyle counselling, Evidence-based treatment protocols, Access to essential medicines and technology, Risk-based CVD management, Team-based care, and Systems for monitoring. It is written for policy-makers and program managers in ministries of health.

Cohort Study

Can a nurse-led community-based model of hypertension care improve hypertension control in Ghana? Results from the ComHIP cohort study

Adler AJ, Laar A, Prieto-Merino D, Der RMM, Mangortey D, Dirks R, Lamptey P, Perel P. BMJ Open. 2019;9(4):e026799. doi:10.1136/bmjopen-2018-026799. PMID: 30944139.
Read the full study →
Study summarySummary
The Community-based Hypertension Improvement Project (ComHIP) tested a community-based, task-sharing model of hypertension care in Lower Manya Krobo, Eastern Region: screening at community points, management by trained community nurses, SMS adherence and refill reminders, and a cloud-based record system. Among the participants seen at one year, 72% had their blood pressure under control, with average reductions of 12.2 mmHg systolic and 7.5 mmHg diastolic. At enrollment, 31% of all participants had it under control; those who stayed had started higher (36% to 43%) than those who never returned (25%). The study had no control group. The study's central limitation was retention: of 1,339 people enrolled, 552 completed a 6-month visit and 338 completed a 12-month visit, and the authors conclude that high loss to follow-up limits the public health impact of these programs.
Relevance to the Initiative: ComHIP is the closest published precedent to the Initiative's work in Ghana, and its lesson is the Initiative's founding premise. Blood pressure control improved among the people who stayed, and most people were lost within the first year. The Initiative's twelve-month closed loop, with structured contact at Days 14, 30, 90, 180, and 365 and a dedicated follow-up coordination team, is designed directly against this documented retention gap.
Qualitative Study

Barriers and facilitators to the implementation of a community-based hypertension improvement project in Ghana: a qualitative study of ComHIP

Adler AJ, Laar AK, Kotoh AM, Legido-Quigley H, Perel P, Lamptey P, Lange IL. BMC Health Services Research. 2020;20(1):67. doi:10.1186/s12913-019-4774-x. PMID: 32000777.
Read the full study →
Study summarySummary
A qualitative implementation study of ComHIP drawing on interviews and focus group discussions with 55 informants: patients, nurses and other health care providers, licensed chemical sellers, and Ghana Health Service staff. Patients and nurses both said the program had strengthened their relationship: nurses were seen as spending more time in consultations, patients came to know their nurses by name, and many nurses reported calling or visiting patients who missed appointments. The documented barriers are equally instructive. Front-line screeners who received training and equipment but no payment reported the lowest satisfaction in the program. Community nurses could not legally dispense antihypertensive medication, which made medication access the participants' largest complaint. And the program's digital record platform was out of service for several months, breaking record linkage and reminder messages.
Relevance to the Initiative: This study reads like a checklist of the Initiative's design decisions. The Initiative budgets compensation for every community health role in funded operations, promises referral rather than dispensing and pairs it with NHIS enrollment support, builds on an offline-capable open-source data platform with redundancy planning, and is designing follow-up so each participant hears from a consistent coordination team.
Qualitative Study

Management of chronic non-communicable diseases in Ghana: a qualitative study using the chronic care model

Amu H, Darteh EKM, Tarkang EE, Kumi-Kyereme A. BMC Public Health. 2021;21(1):1120. doi:10.1186/s12889-021-11170-4. PMID: 34116657.
Read the full study →
Study summarySummary
A qualitative study of 82 patients with chronic non-communicable diseases (hypertension included) and 30 health professionals at Korle Bu and Komfo Anokye Teaching Hospitals, applying the Chronic Care Model framework to identify the practices and challenges shaping CNCD management in Ghana. Patients' accounts centered on self-management: self-restrictions, exercise, and using equipment to monitor their health at home. Inadequate logistics, work-related stress from heavy workload, poor utility supply, and patients' inability to afford the cost of care were the challenges identified.
Relevance to the Initiative: Gives Ghanaian context for the Initiative's closed-loop follow-up design. The Initiative uses the Chronic Care Model to structure twelve-month tracking, and the barriers the study documents (logistics, cost, workforce strain) are ones its community-volunteer model and its links to NHIS and FPHC are designed to ease.
Community Program

May Measurement Month 2022: an analysis of blood pressure screening results from Ghana

Twumasi-Ankrah B, Kiddy-Kodua JO, Aniagyei M, Adu-Adadey M, Gatorwu S, Beaney T, Kerr G, Poulter NR, Osei-Agyemang C, Myers-Hansen GA. European Heart Journal Supplements. 2025;27(Suppl 7):vii30-vii32. doi:10.1093/eurheartjsupp/suaf073. PMID: 40979994.
Read the full study →
Study summarySummary
Results from the 2022 May Measurement Month campaign in Ghana. Medical professionals and volunteers screened 3,654 adults at a marketplace, schools, shopping centers, a bus terminal, community centers, and churches in Accra, Kumasi, Koforidua, and Cape Coast. About one in three (32.0%) had hypertension, and only 10.7% of them were aware of it. Nearly half of everyone screened (45.6%) had never had their blood pressure measured before. People with uncontrolled hypertension were given advice and referred to medical facilities; the authors note that the campaign has no follow-up data.
Relevance to the Initiative: Operational precedent for community screening events in Ghana. It shows how many people with hypertension such events find, and it has no follow-up data, which is the gap the Initiative is built to close.
Ecosystem

Partnerships and Policy Landscape

The Initiative operates within a network of global and Ghanaian organizations working to address hypertension at community and policy levels.

Ghana's hypertension space already includes important and longstanding work by Healthy Heart Africa, the Akomapa project, ADHINCRA, TASSH, the Ghana Heart Initiative, the Ghanaian Society of Cardiology, the World Heart Federation, May Measurement Month Ghana, and others. The Initiative is one contribution to a shared field, not a replacement for any of it.

Stakeholder Proceedings

Addressing the roadblocks to hypertension management in Ghana: proceedings of a roundtable discussion

Doku A, Ahadzi D, Adams EA, Folson AA, Codato E, Agyekum F. Ghana Medical Journal. 2024;58(3):239-244. doi:10.4314/gmj.v58i3.9. PMID: 39398091.
Read the proceedings →
Study summarySummary
Proceedings from the advocacy roundtable of 31 August and 1 September 2022 on hypertension management in Ghana, convened by the World Heart Federation, the Ghanaian Society of Cardiology, and the Stroke Association Support Network (SASNET) Ghana, with co-funding from the Ghana Heart Initiative. The roundtable identified inadequate financing for cardiovascular disease care, insufficient capacity across all levels of the healthcare system, and gaps in patient education as the major roadblocks to hypertension management in Ghana.

Health Systems Research

Community-Based Health Planning and Services Plus programme in Ghana: A qualitative study with stakeholders in two Systems Learning Districts on improving the implementation of primary health care

Kweku M, Amu H, Awolu A, Adjuik M, Ayanore MA, Manu E, et al. PLoS One. 2020;15(1):e0226808. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0226808. PMID: 31914122.
Read the full study →
Study summarySummary
A qualitative study carried out in 2018 with 60 stakeholders in two districts of the Volta Region on Ghana's Community-Based Health Planning and Services (CHPS) programme, the country's key primary health care strategy since 1999. CHPS compounds are among the facilities that deliver the Free Primary Healthcare Programme. The study identifies challenges that inhibit CHPS, among them a lack of community ownership, community members coming for care late, late referrals, and missing logistics, and it names training priorities for health workers, including logistics management, community entry and engagement, emergency delivery, and managing referrals. It proposes continuous community engagement as a way to improve the programme.
Relevance to the Initiative: Peer-reviewed evidence on the level of care where the Free Primary Healthcare Programme is delivered. The study predates the programme and does not mention it. The challenges it identifies at CHPS facilities, including community engagement gaps, referral management, and late care-seeking, are gaps the Initiative's closed loop is designed to help close at the community level.

MedExplain Health is a nonprofit health literacy platform that creates evidence-based, accessible medical information using the Patient Activated Learning System (PALS) methodology. Longitudinal Evidence

Population-Level Research on Ghanaian Health

Ghana's hypertension burden is not one population. Public health research in Ghana distinguishes urban, rural, and peri-urban communities, because their risk profiles, access to care, and follow-up barriers differ. Peri-urban communities sit inside or at the edge of urban areas while living with rural levels of income, services, and infrastructure. The Initiative's population model names these categories explicitly: our screening reaches informal-sector workers in urban commercial spaces, including many who live in peri-urban communities where services are thinnest, and our follow-up design accounts for the barriers each group faces. The two studies below are examples from that research.

Baseline Survey

Evaluation of a community-based hypertension improvement program (ComHIP) in Ghana: data from a baseline survey

Lamptey P, Laar A, Adler AJ, Dirks R, Caldwell A, Prieto-Merino D, Aerts A, Pearce N, Perel P. BMC Public Health. 2017;17(1):368. doi:10.1186/s12889-017-4260-5. PMID: 28454523.
Read the full study →
Study summarySummary
The baseline survey for the ComHIP program: 2,400 adults aged 30 and over interviewed in an intervention district and a comparison district in the Eastern Region. The intervention district, Lower Manya Krobo, is described as peri-urban, with 84% of its residents living in urban areas. The evaluation used a quasi-experimental design: before-and-after surveys in both districts, with a participant cohort nested in the intervention district. At baseline, 32.4% of respondents had hypertension; 46.2% of them were aware of it, and 1.3% had it under control.
Relevance to the Initiative: A Ghanaian example of studying one defined district and naming its setting precisely. The Initiative applies the same discipline at the district level within Greater Accra, serving urban and peri-urban informal-sector workers.
Prospective Cohort

Blood pressure change and hypertension incidence among Ghanaians living in rural Ghana, urban Ghana and The Netherlands: a prospective cohort study

van der Linden EL, Hoevenaar-Blom M, Beune E, Nkansah Darko S, Twumasi Ankrah S, Meeks KAC, Chilunga F, Hayfron-Benjamin C, Henneman P, van den Born BJ, Owusu Dabo E, Agyemang C. eClinicalMedicine. 2025;81:103141. doi:10.1016/j.eclinm.2025.103141. PMID: 40115171.
Read the full study →
Study summarySummary
A prospective cohort study (RODAM-Pros) that measured blood pressure twice, about 6.5 years apart, among Ghanaians living in rural Ghana, urban Ghana, and Amsterdam, and among Dutch residents of Amsterdam. It builds on the original RODAM (Research on Obesity and Diabetes among African Migrants) study by adding longitudinal follow-up. Systolic blood pressure rose more in rural and urban Ghana than among Ghanaian migrants in the Netherlands; the largest rise was among women in rural Ghana (9.5 mmHg).
Relevance to the Initiative: Longitudinal evidence, from a cohort in the Ashanti Region, that blood pressure rose over about six years among adults in rural as well as urban Ghana.
